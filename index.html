What stays behind

  • Body Battery, stress, training load, readiness. Garmin's and Google's own scores. Apple Health has nowhere to put them.
  • Heart-rate variability. Garmin and Google measure it one way (RMSSD), Apple Health another (SDNN). Copying the numbers across would label them wrongly.
  • Exercise minutes, Stand hours, wrist temperature. Apple works these out itself and lets no app add them.

HIT lists exactly which of these are in your export before it writes anything.

What happens inside HIT.

It takes the archive exactly as it arrives

A 133 MB Strava export, a Garmin download, or a folder of files off an old watch. Drop it into HIT through Files, or AirDrop it straight to the app. No unzipping, no renaming, no sorting anything into folders first.

Every file is read before anything is written

HIT parses each track file, works out what the activity actually was, and checks it against Health. Only then does it show you the plan. Files it cannot read are reported rather than silently skipped.

Nothing is written until you have seen the plan

The screen tells you how many are new, how many are already there, and how many Health has from another app and can only be added to. Then you decide.

It tells you how long it will take

A real estimate from the pace of the run in front of you, not a guess made at the start. And it says the true constraint out loud: stay in the app while it runs, because iOS pauses the work if you switch away.

And it tells you what it could not do

Routes attached, routes Health refused, files that would not read, activities that turned out to be planned routes rather than recorded ones. Every number is the real one.

It will not give you two of everything.

This is the part that makes importing years of history bearable. Every activity carries an identity stamp, so HIT can always tell its own work from everyone else's, and tell a re-import from a new one.

What it will not do, and why.

These are Apple's rules, not our choices. Worth knowing before you buy, because nobody can work around them.

  • Replace a workout another app wrote

    Apple only lets an app delete data it saved itself, so a workout from your watch or the Strava app is permanently read-only to HIT. It can be added to, never swapped out.

  • Touch your Move ring

    Health adds up energy and distance from every source. Writing your export's numbers onto a workout another app owns would count them twice, so HIT refuses. Heart rate, cadence and power are not summed, so those are safe to add.

  • Import things that are not workouts

    A Strava export carries saved routes as well as activities. They have coordinates but no timestamps, so they are recognised and left out.

First you need the zip.

This is the part that stops most people, and none of it happens inside HIT. The big exports are free, arrive by email, and can take a few hours.

Exporting from Strava

  1. Open strava.com in a browser and sign in. This cannot be done in the phone app.
  2. Go to Settings › My Account › Download or Delete Your Account.
  3. Under Download Request, press Request Your Archive. You are only asking for the download; nothing is deleted.
  4. Strava emails you a zip, usually within a few hours. A long history takes longer.
  5. Open the email on your iPhone, tap the link, and choose Save to Files. Leave it zipped.

The archive holds an activities.csv alongside a folder of FIT, GPX and TCX files. HIT reads the whole thing as it comes.

Strava's own instructions ›

Exporting from Garmin

  1. Open garmin.com in a browser and sign in.
  2. Go to Account Management › Export Your Data.
  3. Request your data. Garmin emails a link when it is ready.
  4. Download it, then save the zip to Files on your iPhone.
  5. Hand HIT the whole zip. It contains several folders, and HIT finds the workout files inside without being told where they are.

The zip holds far more than workouts: the watch's own minute-by-minute files for heart rate, steps, blood oxygen and breathing, every night's sleep, and your weigh-ins. HIT reads all of it.

Garmin's export page ›

Exporting from Google

  1. Open takeout.google.com in a browser and sign in.
  2. Press Deselect all, then tick Fit and Google Health.
  3. Choose a .zip and the largest file size, so it arrives as one zip rather than several.
  4. Google emails a link when it is ready. Save the zip to Files.

Google Fit's steps and distance and Google Health's heart rate, sleep, blood oxygen and weight all come across. A zip of either folder on its own works too.

FIT Anywhere else

  1. Export the .fit, .gpx or .tcx files from your app or watch. Wahoo, COROS, Suunto, Polar and Zwift all write at least one of these.
  2. Put them in a zip. In folders or loose, it makes no difference.
  3. Save it to Files and hand it to HIT.

Where there is no spreadsheet describing the files, the sport, distance and times are read from the files themselves.

Before you start

How many workouts can it handle?

The reference archive it is developed against holds 1,180 files and 133 MB, and it is imported end to end as part of testing. There is no fixed ceiling.

Can I bring only my workouts, or only my health data?

Yes. HIT reads the whole zip first and shows what is in it: how many workouts, by sport, and how many days and kinds of health data. Then you choose workouts, health data, or both.

Will my steps be counted twice?

No. Your iPhone has probably been counting steps all along. Where Health already has steps, energy, distance or sleep for a stretch of time, from any source, HIT leaves that time alone and fills only the gaps. Nothing is counted twice.

Why isn’t my heart-rate variability copied?

Garmin and Google measure it as RMSSD; Apple Health stores SDNN, a different measure. Copying the numbers across would put them under the wrong name, so HIT leaves them behind and says so.

Can I move to a new Apple account?

Yes. Back up with Health Export Tool on the old account, then import the backup with HIT on the new one. Restoring into the account it came from writes nothing, because everything is already there.

What happens if I run the same export twice?

Nothing. The second run reads the archive, finds every activity and record already in Health, and writes none of them. That is the behaviour the app is built around rather than a side effect.

What if the import is interrupted?

It offers to carry on from where it stopped, which costs one check against Health rather than reading every file again. Nothing is written twice, and an activity Health refused is not retried forever.

Will it mess up my Move ring or my Fitness rings?

No. Health adds up energy and distance from every source, so HIT never writes those onto a workout another app already owns. Workouts it creates itself carry their own figures, exactly as importing them should.

Do I need to unzip the file first?

No, and please do not. Hand HIT the zip as it arrives.

Does it work with an Apple Watch?

There is no watch app. HIT writes into Apple Health on your iPhone, which is where your watch's own workouts already live, so everything ends up in one place.

Can I get my money back?

Purchases go through Apple, so refunds do too. Apple's process is at reportaproblem.apple.com. The free allowance exists so you can see the app work on your own data first.
